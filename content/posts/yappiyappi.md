I wanted a serif that felt hand-drawn and slightly imperfect—something with the warmth of old indie zines rather than rigid editorial typefaces. So I designed **Yappiyappi Serif Regular** (v001.002), an OpenType font built for headlines, posters, packaging, and projects that need a bit of personality[cite: 1].

![Yappiyappi Serif Showcase Poster 1](/p1.png)

## What Makes It Different

Most serifs aim for strict symmetry and razor-sharp terminals. With Yappiyappi Serif, I leaned into soft, rounded edges, uneven stroke weights, and bouncy baselines[cite: 1, 2]. Letters like `y`, `g`, `k`, and `r` carry curved tails that break up standard text blocks[cite: 1, 2].

![Yappiyappi Serif Showcase Poster 2](/p2.png)

It works best at large display sizes where those small quirks show up clearly[cite: 6, 7, 8, 9].
